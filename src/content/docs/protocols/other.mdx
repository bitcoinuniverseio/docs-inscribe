## DUST-20

Deploy, mint and transfer, with one idea at its centre: **the mint output satoshi value
encodes the amount**. The value of the output is not padding, it is data.

That makes the output size a protocol matter rather than a preference, so do not treat the
546 and 330 satoshi choices here the way you would on an ordinary inscription.

Workspace: `/dust20`. Released.

## ChainBloom

**Inscribe only.** A shared world grown one contribution at a time, where each contribution
is an inscription.

ChainBloom is one of the flows that chooses funding outputs on your behalf, so its fee
inputs pass through the asset screening described in
[asset safety](/docs-inscribe/concepts/asset-safety/).

Workspace: `/chainbloom`. Released, and a deployment must switch it on.

## CAT-20

**Not released.** CAT-20 is a covenant token protocol on **Fractal Bitcoin**, a separate
chain from Bitcoin. It is not the FAIR CAT-20 standard on Bitcoin. The `/cat20` workspace
asks you to choose Fractal Mainnet or Fractal Testnet, and never mixes the two.
Direct token links can name the chain with `/cat20/<token-id>?network=fractal-testnet`.

The workspace has three views. **Explore** searches tokens by name, ticker or the full
token ID. **My tokens** lists the CAT-20 balances of your connected Fractal wallet.
**Activity** keeps every order you created on this device, with its status and recovery.
From a token page or a balance you can:

- **Create token.** Deploy a new token with a name, ticker, decimals, maximum supply and
  mint limit. You can reserve an optional premine for your own wallet.
- **Premine.** The deploying wallet issues its reserved premine before public minting opens.
- **Mint.** Mint up to the token's per-mint limit.
- **Send.** Send to up to three recipients in one transaction.
- **Burn.** Destroy part or all of a balance. You must confirm separately that a burn
  cannot be undone.

What the protocol allows, and what Inscribe therefore enforces:

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Decimals when creating a token | 0 to 9 |
| Largest amount in one output | 2,147,483,647 base units |
| Maximum supply and premine | Whole multiples of the mint limit |
| Token inputs per transaction | Up to 4 |
| Recipients per send | Up to 3, plus your change |

Each action shows a review before your wallet signs. The review lists every transaction
in the order, what each one spends and creates, and its fee. Your wallet signs; Inscribe
never holds your key. Before any transaction is sent, Inscribe rebuilds each one from the
reviewed plan and rejects signed bytes that differ from it.

An order reported as broadcast has only been submitted. Confirmation and CAT-20 indexing
are checked separately, so a submitted transfer is not yet a credited balance. An encrypted
recovery kit restores access to your orders after a reload; connecting a wallet alone does
not.

Inscribe builds and checks every transaction against Universe's own Fractal node, CAT-20
tracker and funding inventory. If it cannot verify what a transaction spends, it refuses
to build it and says why when you try. A CAT tracker that is only catching up does not
block minting. Universe does not use third-party Fractal data
services, including as a fallback.

Workspaces: `/cat20` and `/fractal`. Both are **not released** on Fractal Mainnet.

## RGB

**Not released.** RGB keeps asset state off chain, in a private stock that only the
owner holds; Bitcoin carries a commitment to each state change inside an ordinary
transaction. An unreleased repair candidate runs RGB through a Universe-operated
companion (rgb-api 0.11.1, the stable RGB line) rather than in the browser or with an
in-memory stand-in.

What the candidate does, on Bitcoin Signet:

- Issues NIA, CFA, UDA, IFA and PFA contracts and imports contracts other people issue.
- Creates invoices, prepares transfers and returns the witness transaction as a PSBT.
  **Your wallet signs it**; the companion never holds a Bitcoin key. The signed
  transaction is accepted only when its id matches the reviewed PSBT.
- Validates and accepts consignments natively and records an acceptance receipt the
  sender can attach to the transfer.
- Backs the companion stock up as an encrypted envelope (AES-256-GCM, bound to your
  wallet and chain) in the hosted vault, and restores it after device loss.

Two limits are stated rather than hidden: PFA transfers need an issuer signature the
candidate does not collect yet, so they are reported unavailable; and a transfer is only
complete after its witness confirms and the recipient accepts, so a broadcast on its own
never counts.

Workspace: `/rgb`. Not released. Mainnet execution requires an explicit operator
authorization and has not run; this candidate does not establish a deployed release.

## RGB++ and Fiber

**Not released on mainnet.** RGB++ binds an asset cell on Nervos CKB to a Bitcoin UTXO: a
Bitcoin transaction carries the commitment, the CKB side is unlocked with a Bitcoin SPV proof.
Fiber is a Lightning-style payment channel network on CKB. An unreleased candidate replaces
the earlier non-functional workspace with native execution on the pinned rgbpp-sdk 0.7.4 and Fiber v0.9.1.

What the candidate does, on the Bitcoin Testnet3 + CKB Pudge tuple:

- Reads the RGB++ assets bound to your Bitcoin address and verifies a binding on both chains
  (Bitcoin confirmation, CKB cell, lock and type match, SPV proof availability).
- Issues xUDT and Spore assets, sends assets on Bitcoin and leaps them to CKB and back. The
  operator funds the CKB launch cell and acts as paymaster; **your wallet signs the Bitcoin
  transaction** and the API accepts the signed transaction only when it equals the reviewed
  plan. A BTC→CKB leap becomes spendable on CKB after six Bitcoin confirmations (the RGB++
  BTC time lock); the bridge view shows that maturity and nothing else is invented.
- Opens and closes Fiber channels from the Universe node, creates and pays node-encoded
  invoices, reads settlement from the node, and lists uncooperative closes with their CKB
  settlement transaction. Cross-chain hub orders (Lightning ↔ Fiber) are created by the node
  and refund by HTLC timeout when they fail.

Every operation is persisted per wallet and network with an idempotency key, so a reload or
reconnect resumes it from Bitcoin, CKB and Fiber readback rather than from memory.

Workspace: `/rgbpp-fiber`. Not released. Mainnet needs Universe-owned CKB, Fiber and RGB++
proof services that do not exist yet; the deployed health route names that blocker instead of
reporting readiness.

## Nostr Studio

**Released on mainnet, 17 September 2026.** Nostr is a relay protocol for signed messages:
your key signs an event in the browser, a relay stores it, and other people read it back.
The Studio (`/nostr`) runs against a Universe-owned relay and a Universe-owned Lightning node;
no third-party relay or payment service sits in the path.

What it does:

- **Identity.** Generate or import a key (it stays in this browser's session storage) or use a
  NIP-07 signer extension. Publish a profile, a relay list and an inbox relay list; verify a
  NIP-05 address against its domain.
- **Publishing.** Notes, long-form articles and classified listings, each signed in the page.
  An event counts only after the relay acknowledges it and the Studio reads the exact bytes
  back; the receipt says `published`, `reconciled` (already held) or `superseded` (the relay
  keeps another version of a replaceable event).
- **Messages.** Private messages as NIP-17 gift wraps encrypted in the browser (NIP-44 v2); a
  copy is wrapped for you so the conversation survives a reload. Decryption uses your local
  key or your extension; the server never sees plaintext.
- **Lightning.** Claim a Lightning address (`name@inscribe.bitcoinuniverse.io`), receive zaps
  (NIP-57 receipts are signed by the Studio's zap signer after the owned node settles the
  invoice), pay for a listing with an invoice bound to your order, connect a wallet through
  Nostr Wallet Connect (NIP-47) with a daily budget, and let the Studio zap an author's new
  notes automatically.

What it does not do: a receipt proves the relay holds what you signed, never that a statement
is true. Wallet Connect budgets and auto-zap rules spend from the owned node's ledger; the
node's own liquidity, not a promise, decides whether a payment to the outside can be routed.
Health (`/api/nostr/health`) reports the relay, database, node and worker state separately
and is READY only when all four are.

Workspace: `/nostr`. Route contract and acceptance evidence: the `docs/nostr-domain-routes.md`
and `docs/repair-nostr-20260917.md` files of the application repository.

## Workspaces that are not finished

Four more workspaces exist in the app and are classified as incomplete by the production
contract. They are listed here so their absence from the rest of this site is a stated
decision rather than an oversight:

| Workspace | What it is meant to become |
| --- | --- |
| **Witness Circles** | Taproot outputs a group authorises together |
| **Tandem** | Building and changing one object together, with a signed record of each change |
| **Patina** | How long a value-carrying output has stayed where it is |
| **Drop Pacts** | Designing and launching Drop Pact agreements, with a resumable tutorial |

None of these are documented on this site as things you can rely on. Tandem does have one
effect worth knowing even so: **Tandem state carriers cannot fund ordinary sends, mints,
replacements or recovery plans at all**, which is a firmer restriction than the usual asset
screening applies.
