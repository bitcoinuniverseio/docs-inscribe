DUST-20

Section titled “DUST-20”

Deploy, mint and transfer, with one idea at its centre: the mint output satoshi value encodes the amount. The value of the output is not padding, it is data.

That makes the output size a protocol matter rather than a preference, so do not treat the 546 and 330 satoshi choices here the way you would on an ordinary inscription.

Workspace: /dust20. Released.

ChainBloom

Section titled “ChainBloom”

Inscribe only. A shared world grown one contribution at a time, where each contribution is an inscription.

ChainBloom is one of the flows that chooses funding outputs on your behalf, so its fee inputs pass through the asset screening described in asset safety.

Workspace: /chainbloom. Released, and a deployment must switch it on.

CAT-20

Section titled “CAT-20”

Not released. CAT-20 is a covenant token protocol on Fractal Bitcoin, a separate chain from Bitcoin. It is not the FAIR CAT-20 standard on Bitcoin. The /cat20 workspace asks you to choose Fractal Mainnet or Fractal Testnet, and never mixes the two. Direct token links can name the chain with /cat20/<token-id>?network=fractal-testnet.

The workspace has three views. Explore searches tokens by name, ticker or the full token ID. My tokens lists the CAT-20 balances of your connected Fractal wallet. Activity keeps every order you created on this device, with its status and recovery. From a token page or a balance you can:

  • Create token. Deploy a new token with a name, ticker, decimals, maximum supply and mint limit. You can reserve an optional premine for your own wallet.
  • Premine. The deploying wallet issues its reserved premine before public minting opens.
  • Mint. Mint up to the token’s per-mint limit.
  • Send. Send to up to three recipients in one transaction.
  • Burn. Destroy part or all of a balance. You must confirm separately that a burn cannot be undone.

What the protocol allows, and what Inscribe therefore enforces:

LimitValue
Decimals when creating a token0 to 9
Largest amount in one output2,147,483,647 base units
Maximum supply and premineWhole multiples of the mint limit
Token inputs per transactionUp to 4
Recipients per sendUp to 3, plus your change

Each action shows a review before your wallet signs. The review lists every transaction in the order, what each one spends and creates, and its fee. Your wallet signs; Inscribe never holds your key. Before any transaction is sent, Inscribe rebuilds each one from the reviewed plan and rejects signed bytes that differ from it.

An order reported as broadcast has only been submitted. Confirmation and CAT-20 indexing are checked separately, so a submitted transfer is not yet a credited balance. An encrypted recovery kit restores access to your orders after a reload; connecting a wallet alone does not.

Inscribe builds and checks every transaction against Universe’s own Fractal node, CAT-20 tracker and funding inventory. If it cannot verify what a transaction spends, it refuses to build it and says why when you try. A CAT tracker that is only catching up does not block minting. Universe does not use third-party Fractal data services, including as a fallback.

Workspaces: /cat20 and /fractal. Both are not released on Fractal Mainnet.

RGB

Section titled “RGB”

Not released. RGB keeps asset state off chain, in a private stock that only the owner holds; Bitcoin carries a commitment to each state change inside an ordinary transaction. An unreleased repair candidate runs RGB through a Universe-operated companion (rgb-api 0.11.1, the stable RGB line) rather than in the browser or with an in-memory stand-in.

What the candidate does, on Bitcoin Signet:

  • Issues NIA, CFA, UDA, IFA and PFA contracts and imports contracts other people issue.
  • Creates invoices, prepares transfers and returns the witness transaction as a PSBT. Your wallet signs it; the companion never holds a Bitcoin key. The signed transaction is accepted only when its id matches the reviewed PSBT.
  • Validates and accepts consignments natively and records an acceptance receipt the sender can attach to the transfer.
  • Backs the companion stock up as an encrypted envelope (AES-256-GCM, bound to your wallet and chain) in the hosted vault, and restores it after device loss.

Two limits are stated rather than hidden: PFA transfers need an issuer signature the candidate does not collect yet, so they are reported unavailable; and a transfer is only complete after its witness confirms and the recipient accepts, so a broadcast on its own never counts.

Workspace: /rgb. Not released. Mainnet execution requires an explicit operator authorization and has not run; this candidate does not establish a deployed release.

RGB++ and Fiber

Section titled “RGB++ and Fiber”

Not released on mainnet. RGB++ binds an asset cell on Nervos CKB to a Bitcoin UTXO: a Bitcoin transaction carries the commitment, the CKB side is unlocked with a Bitcoin SPV proof. Fiber is a Lightning-style payment channel network on CKB. An unreleased candidate replaces the earlier non-functional workspace with native execution on the pinned rgbpp-sdk 0.7.4 and Fiber v0.9.1.

What the candidate does, on the Bitcoin Testnet3 + CKB Pudge tuple:

  • Reads the RGB++ assets bound to your Bitcoin address and verifies a binding on both chains (Bitcoin confirmation, CKB cell, lock and type match, SPV proof availability).
  • Issues xUDT and Spore assets, sends assets on Bitcoin and leaps them to CKB and back. The operator funds the CKB launch cell and acts as paymaster; your wallet signs the Bitcoin transaction and the API accepts the signed transaction only when it equals the reviewed plan. A BTC→CKB leap becomes spendable on CKB after six Bitcoin confirmations (the RGB++ BTC time lock); the bridge view shows that maturity and nothing else is invented.
  • Opens and closes Fiber channels from the Universe node, creates and pays node-encoded invoices, reads settlement from the node, and lists uncooperative closes with their CKB settlement transaction. Cross-chain hub orders (Lightning ↔ Fiber) are created by the node and refund by HTLC timeout when they fail.

Every operation is persisted per wallet and network with an idempotency key, so a reload or reconnect resumes it from Bitcoin, CKB and Fiber readback rather than from memory.

Workspace: /rgbpp-fiber. Not released. Mainnet needs Universe-owned CKB, Fiber and RGB++ proof services that do not exist yet; the deployed health route names that blocker instead of reporting readiness.

Nostr Studio

Section titled “Nostr Studio”

Released on mainnet, 17 September 2026. Nostr is a relay protocol for signed messages: your key signs an event in the browser, a relay stores it, and other people read it back. The Studio (/nostr) runs against a Universe-owned relay and a Universe-owned Lightning node; no third-party relay or payment service sits in the path.

What it does:

  • Identity. Generate or import a key (it stays in this browser’s session storage) or use a NIP-07 signer extension. Publish a profile, a relay list and an inbox relay list; verify a NIP-05 address against its domain.
  • Publishing. Notes, long-form articles and classified listings, each signed in the page. An event counts only after the relay acknowledges it and the Studio reads the exact bytes back; the receipt says published, reconciled (already held) or superseded (the relay keeps another version of a replaceable event).
  • Messages. Private messages as NIP-17 gift wraps encrypted in the browser (NIP-44 v2); a copy is wrapped for you so the conversation survives a reload. Decryption uses your local key or your extension; the server never sees plaintext.
  • Lightning. Claim a Lightning address (name@inscribe.bitcoinuniverse.io), receive zaps (NIP-57 receipts are signed by the Studio’s zap signer after the owned node settles the invoice), pay for a listing with an invoice bound to your order, connect a wallet through Nostr Wallet Connect (NIP-47) with a daily budget, and let the Studio zap an author’s new notes automatically.

What it does not do: a receipt proves the relay holds what you signed, never that a statement is true. Wallet Connect budgets and auto-zap rules spend from the owned node’s ledger; the node’s own liquidity, not a promise, decides whether a payment to the outside can be routed. Health (/api/nostr/health) reports the relay, database, node and worker state separately and is READY only when all four are.

Workspace: /nostr. Route contract and acceptance evidence: the docs/nostr-domain-routes.md and docs/repair-nostr-20260917.md files of the application repository.

Taproot Assets

Section titled “Taproot Assets”

Released on mainnet on 2026-09-26. Taproot Assets are tokens and collectibles issued on Bitcoin, recorded in Taproot outputs and carried with a proof file. With a Taproot wallet you can mint (paid from your own wallet, directly or through Composer), hold, receive, send and prove assets and browse issuance records. On Signet, mints and sends can also be paid by Universe, and you can pay Lightning invoices from a hosted asset balance; asset Lightning is not offered on mainnet yet. Every asset is locked to your wallet’s key, and nothing is published before you approve it.

Workspace: /taproot-assets. The full guide, including what you sign, who pays the Bitcoin fee and what cannot be undone, is Taproot Assets.

Workspaces that are not finished

Section titled “Workspaces that are not finished”

Four more workspaces exist in the app and are classified as incomplete by the production contract. They are listed here so their absence from the rest of this site is a stated decision rather than an oversight:

WorkspaceWhat it is meant to become
Witness CirclesTaproot outputs a group authorises together
TandemBuilding and changing one object together, with a signed record of each change
PatinaHow long a value-carrying output has stayed where it is
Drop PactsDesigning and launching Drop Pact agreements, with a resumable tutorial

None of these are documented on this site as things you can rely on. Tandem does have one effect worth knowing even so: Tandem state carriers cannot fund ordinary sends, mints, replacements or recovery plans at all, which is a firmer restriction than the usual asset screening applies.

Spark

Section titled “Spark”

Spark is a Bitcoin layer run by a small set of signing operators. Tokens on Spark move instantly and cost no network fee. Your Spark wallet has its own keys; they stay encrypted in your browser and never reach Universe.

What the workspace does:

  • Browse every Spark token, with no wallet needed. Universe reads the operators’ full token record into its own database, so the list covers all tokens, not a curated set. Each token shows its name, ticker, decimals, supply cap, what has been minted and burned, and whether the issuer can freeze it. The page says how current the list is and never shows a partial list as complete.

  • My tokens: hold, send and receive tokens with exact amounts. Amounts use the token’s decimals and are never rounded.

  • Create & manage: create a token (name, ticker, decimals, cap and freezing are permanent), then mint, burn, freeze and unfreeze it. Only the issuer can mint, and minted tokens go to the issuer wallet; sending them is a separate step.

  • Activity: an action waiting for confirmation is checked automatically. Nothing is ever sent twice. If the network never received an action, the page says so once it is safe to be sure.

  • Bitcoin: deposit bitcoin to your Spark wallet and claim it, send it to another Spark address, request an exact amount, pay a Spark request, and lock a payment to a secret (HTLC) that returns to you if it is not claimed in time.

  • Leave without the operators (Advanced, Exit kit): if the operators stop responding, the exit kit builds the transactions that take your bitcoin back on chain. You pay the fee from a Bitcoin coin you control, submit each step from your own Bitcoin Core 29 or newer node, and wait out each timelock. Exit sweep then sends the bitcoin to any address. Your Spark balance stops working once the first step confirms.

Every action is reviewed in plain words before you sign. Token actions were verified end to end on Spark’s public test network, and bitcoin actions and the exit on a Spark network run by Universe. Withdrawals to a Bitcoin address, Lightning, and Flashnet trades depend on outside services that could not be tested, so they stay off on mainnet.

Workspace: /spark.
